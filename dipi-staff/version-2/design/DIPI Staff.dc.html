WHAT CHANGED — AND WHY
01
The hub became a board
Eighteen equal tiles gave every job the same weight. Now four live numbers carry the navigation, three verb-first rows say what to do next, and the twelve PDFs drop to small type — they are exports, not decisions.
02
One roll, not two tables
Unattended and Attended were separate tables, so you scanned twice and lost your place. It is one list now: check someone in and the row keeps its position, gains a tick and shows room · seat.
03
Audit grouped by check, not by person
Red / orange / yellow squares told you nothing about the work. The list is now the check that fired with the count beside it — and where the fix is mechanical, one button clears all of them.
04
Room picking stays in the dialog
Choosing a room was a whole other screen. It now opens inside check-in, pre-filtered to free rooms of the right gender, so one arrival is one dialog and never a round trip.
05
Calls log an outcome
A column of identical blue call buttons has no memory of who answered. Reached / No answer / Call back sit on the row, the pile empties as you work, and the header counts the round.
06
Colour carries meaning again
Amenity chips were blue, green and purple for no reason. Steel is now the only colour, and it means one thing: this is live, occupied or selected. Everything else is a hairline drawing.
07
A bug your sketch caught
Priya Shah carries conf no NM041 with an F badge. That disagreement is now its own audit check — conf_gender_mismatch — and the roll marks her row with a dot.
